*The 808s are being tortured. The 808s sound like Addison Rae in that Charli xcx song. The 808s hit like a raygun to the testicles. The 808s are tearing apart the time-space continuum. The 808s feel like drowning in a volcano, my skin peeling off as the visage of Satan reveals itself.*

The joke was funny the first 100 to 200 times, but it’s becoming a little old. Everything began a few years ago when a few underground producers started pushing plugg rap bass to [blistering](https://soundcloud.com/wildkarduno/introvert) [extremes](https://soundcloud.com/gielaa_archive/he-gon-die). The sound gradually leaked across social media, buoyed by rappers like [LAZER DIM 700](https://pitchfork.com/artists/lazer-dim-700/) and TikTok one-offs like yuke’s “ [ian goin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5CMfzGy_QPc),” which I called one of 2024’s [Songs of the Summer](https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/2024-song-of-the-summer/). Recently, outsiders have zeroed in on the [comedic aspect](https://www.tiktok.com/@auxmit/video/7400222060921834782), spawning a “Worst 808s Ever” trend that turns the style into a [meme](https://www.tiktok.com/@4m.form/video/7503767261241527582). Every comment section is packed with riffs on the [same basic concept](https://www.tiktok.com/@tds5563/video/7505648468959005983): *These 808s are fucking psychotic.* The word “808” has become a kind of catchall for all sorts of warped lowend and screeching synths in beats, even when the drum isn’t actually an 808. The sound originally came from the Roland TR-808 drum machine, and became associated with thick, thudding kicks used in a slew of genres.

Essentially, people are challenging themselves to concoct the most atrociously belligerent bass in history. It’s an arsenal of [cyborgian IBS-distress](https://www.tiktok.com/@capitalbxby/video/7500282861992480046) and [demonic eruptions](https://www.tiktok.com/@prodvmaia8765/video/7320122916790619434). Some of it has genuinely fun, startling sound design. But just as much is [pure TikTok bait](https://www.tiktok.com/@jvcxedits/video/7503422835881168174). The style has basically become one long, viral [punchline](https://www.tiktok.com/@sillybillyahhmusic/video/7509557314664418582), all [disposable flecks](https://www.tiktok.com/@yarryfis/video/7510808995146550574) of sound puked up so people can quip things like “the 808s are speaking in morse code, *har har* ” and [soundtrack *Roblox* edits](https://www.tiktok.com/@heisenberge72/video/7510611282760961302). One of the biggest recent TikToks is a bass sound that’s just the [N-word repeatedly](https://www.tiktok.com/@deluxe_aux/video/7504672078038109470).

This distorted, ultra-abrasive strain of plugg isn’t the first underground rap subgenre to be taken over by cornballs with little interest in the actual scene. There have always been carpetbaggers in rap, people who’ve taken the music outside of its original context and diluted it for a wider, less culturally invested audience. Social media has greatly intensified this process; peep a scene like [phonk](https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/how-phonk-became-the-most-lucrative-yet-lifeless-genre-of-the-2020s/), which devolved from ‘90s Memphis rap homage to a sewage stream of electronic beats to lift weights and mew to. The internet’s global reach has allowed for never-before-heard musical weirdness but also a new kind of accelerated enshittification, where hyperspecific scenes get flattened into trashy aesthetics.

I spoke to perc40, an Atlantan responsible for all kinds of percussive havoc, like wildkarduno’s “ [i dont give a fuxk bout da 808s,](https://soundcloud.com/wildkarduno/i-dont-give-a-fuxk-bout-da)” which he produced with utrippin. He was one of the early [extremo-plugg](https://soundcloud.com/newunderground2/osamason-catch-me-outside-boolymon-marrgielaa) architects along with beatmaker/rappers like Squillo, [tdf](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/tdf-culture/), marrgielaa and boolymon. perc40 told me he finds the 808s trend “weird”; with a cringe in his voice, he said most of these trendbouncers don’t put any effort into sculpting the music besides the provocative bass. “It sounds played out. Minus the 808, the beat is just kinda…” his voice trailed off.

For the plugg heads, there’s a clear intent to the music’s delirium. perc40 lit up as he told me about how he enjoyed experimenting with Ableton’s saturation effects, and the way dreamy melodies clash excitingly with serrated low end on a track like [“pouring up”](https://soundcloud.com/perc40/ohsxnta-wildkarduno-pouring-up-prod-perc40) from his last tape. Other artists toy with the style by throwing [heavenly choral calls](https://soundcloud.com/vrwv/boolymon-cyber-truck-p-reik) over the mix and trying to [match the madness](https://soundcloud.com/2slimey4eva/saddeststory-pvlor5k) with equally aggro threats and growls. These are producers and performers who came from the underground, building on a lineage of influences—from Slayworld to BeatPluggz to RonnyJ’s fried detonations. By focusing solely on “insane 808s,” the context disappears. “When people hyperfixate on the 808s and shit, I feel like it takes away from the rest of the beat,” perc40 said. “And it also takes away from where we got \[the sound\] from.”

When the sound is calibrated correctly, it’s genuinely thrilling, forcing you to retrain your fight-or-flight response to what initially hits like [air raid alarms](https://soundcloud.com/wildkarduno/noticket) and 100db construction clamor. There’s something bizarrely blissful about listening to this music with headphones on, letting the industrial uproar pulverize your brain to bits. And the best producers give their bass such textural [personality](https://soundcloud.com/twovrt/boolymon-jaydes-prod-twovrt) it feels like it’s fighting to get a word in, practically a second vocal feature. tdf’s beat on wildkarduno’s [“all good”](https://soundcloud.com/iloveyou888/wildkarduno-all-good-prod-tdf) sounds like the opposite of *all good*: imagine a scuba diver desperately gasping for air while trapped underwater in the Mariana trench.

On TikTok and X, where the 808 craze has really [taken off](https://www.tiktok.com/@joonaeetukvartti/video/7498799147496000790), many of the songs don’t include vocals and are basically just 20 seconds of a DAW with humorous on-screen text. There’s a mixture of [artful lunacy](https://x.com/selasiii/status/1909652118694244401) that [smacks](https://www.tiktok.com/@4m.form/video/7506232095786765598) like [experimental IDM](https://www.tiktok.com/@michaelcataofficial/video/7507762699506568494) (Insane Dance Music)—especially by people with a background in [electronic sound design](https://www.tiktok.com/@sylvorian/video/7507364506335497515) like [selasi](https://soundcloud.com/selasii/run-that-back) —and [irredeemable](https://www.tiktok.com/@swedish_caredits_2/video/7505150670551387414) [blather](https://www.tiktok.com/@kmrclips/video/7402769079813049630). My ears and eyes are still sore after imbibing TikTok’s [endless stream](https://www.tiktok.com/@heyh0_o/video/7503409930703998251) of low-effort [20,000 Hz sirens](https://www.tiktok.com/@sylvorian/video/7508111148827348270) and “808s screaming from pain” jokes. It’ll be intriguing to see if this rash of outside interest leads to more electronic producers working with plugg rappers. As of now, it feels mostly like people treating the sound as a novelty, a viral “challenge” they can hop on for engagement. This has [angered folks](https://www.tiktok.com/@savemeslime/video/7497760319654186286): “Making music for teenagers posting tiktok content about how chatgpt made them fail English,” the producer dollmaker [wrote](https://x.com/909kick/status/1922889416546164855). “No groove and no love for the game.”

In the grand scheme of things, the distorto-plugg takeover isn’t *that* deep—these two cultural bubbles (comedic 808 extremism and actual rap music) should be able to coexist in harmony. But it feels worse when you look at how so many parts of underground rap have already become washed out. One classic case: “Slowed + reverb” is just gentrified chopped and screwed. Look at Shed Theory, the all-white crew of plugg droners who spawn “nod pits” at their shows and pal around with questionable figures like comedian Sam Hyde. There’s Odetari, who’s managed to sell a sickly Temu brew of underground aesthetics like sigilkore to a mass audience of 10 million monthly listeners. I can’t even tell what’s going on with 9ENSYOKO, the posse of artists who rap in cutesy AI-generated voices over fried beats, with a whole Touhou aesthetic of cartoon anime girls sipping lean and toting guns in Instagram Reels… but it seems offensive. And then there’s the manic spawn of white rappers becoming underground icons, from Brennan Jones’ Glokk40Spaz cosplay to more respectable artists like [ian](https://pitchfork.com/artists/ian/) and [Nettspend](https://pitchfork.com/artists/nettspend/), who started in the underworld but have since joined major labels and tempered their sound for mass appeal.

The “underground” has become such a coherent entity that it’s basically now a commercialized sideshow to the mainstream, requiring its own cast of marketable (and hateable) personas. The people who rise to the top are often the most polarizing yet sufficiently listenable figures, the ones who alienate half the audience while turning the others into addicts. They’re white people mumbling about sipping lean over state-of-the-art production. They’re oddballs who make perfect fodder for high-metric IG posts. Certain artists develop cult online fanbases that don’t give a shit about the underground beyond their idols. As Jane Remover recently told Emwell, host of the underground interview series “Well Well Well”: “My fans are kind of like Opium fans, if they didn't listen to Black people.”

One remedy to this is pretty simple: curiosity. [Dig](https://soundcloud.com/20mop/boolymon-wops-prod-20mop) below the [surface](https://soundcloud.com/onetdf/wildkarduno-20-mins-feat). Resist imbibing the musical equivalent of slop bowls and drink from the subterranean spring. It’s likely that if magma-bass plugg ever notches a hit in the mainstream, it’ll come through a marketable face, an opportunist with pop instincts, not someone who’s been in the scene. But those guys are still there—the tdfs and twovrts, the 2slimeys and Smokingskuls. They’re making some of their most electrifying music now: bass pummeling like a Thwomp to the brain but also [smooth](https://soundcloud.com/snakechildpain/boolymon-bologna-prod-boolymon), cosmic rap that’s the polar opposite of a meme. Take a [sip](https://soundcloud.com/squillooo-management/sets/squillo-album) from the [source](https://soundcloud.com/onetdf/sets/culture).
